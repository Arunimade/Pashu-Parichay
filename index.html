India's Bovine Heritage

India is home to the world's largest cattle population, with a rich diversity of indigenous breeds shaped by millennia of selective breeding, climate adaptation, and cultural significance.

Genetic Diversity

India's cattle breeds represent one of the most genetically diverse populations globally, adapted to tropical heat, monsoon humidity, and varied terrain across 28 states.

Cultural Significance

Cows hold a sacred place in Indian culture and religion. Many indigenous breeds are preserved not just for economic value but as living cultural and spiritual heritage.

Conservation Efforts

ICAR and NBAGR are working to conserve rare breeds through germplasm banks, genomic characterization, and breed registration under the NBAGR system.

Milk Production

Indian zebu cattle produce A2 milk, rich in beta-casein A2 protein, which research suggests has multiple health benefits over the A1 milk produced by Western breeds.

Farmer Livelihoods

Over 70% of Indian farmers depend on livestock for supplementary income. Indigenous breeds are low-maintenance, disease-resistant, and economically viable for smallholders.

Global Influence

Indian zebu breeds like Gir, Kankrej, and Ongole have been exported worldwide and form the genetic base of tropical cattle breeds in Brazil, USA, and Australia.
